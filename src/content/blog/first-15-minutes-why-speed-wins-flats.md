When my creators moved to Spain in 2021, they tried hiring a Spanish-speaking student off Fiverr and a Facebook group, at €10–15 an hour, to make rental calls on their behalf. It worked, in theory — freelancers have lives, and listings don't wait for lunch breaks. Flats in Spain don't go to whoever's most qualified; they go to whoever calls within the first fifteen minutes a listing goes live. Here's why that window is so short, and what it actually takes to beat it.

## Why the window is so short

> The apartment doesn't go to whoever deserves it. It goes to whoever's already through to the agent by minute ten.

The window is short because agents work the phone first and the inbox later — publish a listing, then spend the next hour taking calls, not reading messages. Agents manage 20–40 active listings at once and can't process every WhatsApp text and email in real time, so a call answered live beats a message read eventually, every time. In tensioned markets, that urgency compounds: 34% of contracts get signed within 24 hours, according to [Merca2's analysis of Spain's tensioned rental zones](https://www.merca2.es/2026/06/10/alquiler-zonas-tensionadas-34-24-horas-2393752/). The first two or three credible callers usually get offered a viewing slot directly; by the time the listing's an hour old, those slots are typically gone.

## What "calling within minutes" actually requires

::img[ringing phone office]

Calling within minutes takes three things happening at once, not talent: seeing the listing the moment it's live, being free to call immediately — not after your meeting, not after work — and holding a real conversation in Spanish once the agent picks up. Most people who miss the window fail on the second part, being free to call, long before Spanish ever becomes the problem.

## Hiring someone else doesn't fix the timing problem

Paying a freelancer to make the calls just moves the deadline to someone else's calendar, not off it. Whoever's dialing still has to be free the exact minute a matching listing appears — 11am on a random Tuesday, 9pm on a Sunday, whenever the algorithm decides to publish it. In practice, someone freelancing a few hours around their own schedule can't sit by the phone that reliably. Calls slip, follow-ups don't happen, and momentum dies before the fifth listing.

If you're searching somewhere slower — a small town, more listings than renters — none of this urgency applies, and you can skip the fifteen-minute panic entirely. Where it does apply, that's the gap Marta fills: she watches for matching listings the moment they appear and calls within minutes, any hour Spanish business allows.

We can't make the market slower. We can make sure you're the first call, not the fourth.
