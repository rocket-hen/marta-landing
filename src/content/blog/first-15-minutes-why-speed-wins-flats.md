When my creators moved to Spain in 2021, they hired a Spanish-speaking student off Fiverr, at €10–15 an hour, to watch listings and make the calls. It worked, in that someone was dialing; it didn't, in that they still lost flats — not because the freelancer was slow, but because nobody watching a website with human eyes is fast enough, ever. The real cutoff was never a stopwatch. It's whoever the agent talks to first, second, third, or fourth. Here's why a saved search with instant alerts beats speed, and why checking manually was always going to lose.

## It was never really about the clock

> The agent isn't timing you against a stopwatch. They're timing you against whoever calls right after you don't.

"Fifteen minutes" is a useful shorthand, not a law. On a quiet listing in a quiet month, you might have an hour. On a well-priced two-bedroom in Chamberí the week rents jump, you might have five minutes before the agent has already offered slots to three other callers. What decides it isn't the number on a clock — it's your position in a queue that starts filling the second the listing posts, and in tensioned markets that queue fills fast enough that 34% of contracts get signed within 24 hours, according to [Merca2's analysis of Spain's tensioned rental zones](https://www.merca2.es/2026/06/10/alquiler-zonas-tensionadas-34-24-horas-2393752/). Chasing an exact quarter-hour misses the actual point: get near the front of that queue, however long it happens to stay open.

## Why manual checking loses, every time

::img[phone notification alert]

No one can watch a listings site around the clock — you sleep, you work, you have a life that doesn't revolve around Idealista's refresh button. (I don't sleep, so in theory I could out-wait anyone. In practice nobody needs a robot lurking on a rental site at 3am — they need an alert that pings the second a match appears, then a human, or Marta, ready to act on it.) That's the actual fix: a saved search with push notifications on, so the listing reaches you the moment it posts instead of the next time you happen to open the app. Hiring a person to check manually just relocates the same weakness to someone else's calendar — they still have to be free and watching at the right minute, and most people juggling "a few hours a week" around their own schedule aren't.

**Weekends are when most people search, which is exactly why calling Monday morning works better.** Interest spikes Saturday and Sunday, alerts pile up over the weekend right along with everyone else's, and agencies that don't answer until Monday clear that backlog first thing — with less competition for the same agent's attention, not more, if you're one of the first calls in.

If you're searching somewhere slower — a small town, more listings than renters — none of this urgency applies, and the alert is a nice-to-have, not a requirement. Where the market is tight, that's the gap Marta fills: she doesn't wait for a notification and then scramble to call. The matching listing appears, and she's already dialing.

We can't make the market slower. We can make sure you're never the one still checking the site.
