Bilbao gets pitched as the practical, less-touristy alternative to San Sebastián — real city, real jobs, the Guggenheim instead of a beach economy — but most cost-of-living content treats it as an afterthought next to Madrid and Barcelona. A single person here spends around €765 a month excluding rent. This breaks down what it actually costs to live here, then walks through the neighborhoods, from the tourist-dense Casco Viejo to the quieter hillside of Uribarri, that change that number.

## Monthly budget at a glance: single person vs. family of four

Excluding rent, a single person in Bilbao spends around €765 a month and a family of four around €2,790, according to Numbeo — comfortably below Madrid or Barcelona, and [close to San Sebastián's numbers](/blog/cost-of-living-in-san-sebastian/) despite meaningfully cheaper property. Rent citywide runs roughly €11.50-14.60/m² depending on the source, averaging close to €1,552 a month for a typical apartment.

> Bilbao doesn't have San Sebastián's beach-town price tag. It has the same Basque quality of life without paying Spain's highest property prices for it.

One honest line item worth budgeting for: Bilbao gets meaningfully more rain than the Mediterranean and southern cities in this series, which means real heating costs in a wetter, cooler climate rather than the summer air-conditioning bills that dominate in Seville or Málaga.

## Groceries, dining, transport, and the weather factor

::img[pintxos bar counter Bilbao]

Groceries run €200-300 a month per person. Pintxos — Bilbao's version of tapas, eaten standing at a bar counter — run €2-4 each, and a proper pintxos crawl for two comes out closer to €30-50 depending on how many stops. A monthly transport pass for the metro and bus network runs around €40-50. Utilities run higher here than in the south, closer to €120-160 a month, with heating rather than air conditioning driving the winter bill.

## Casco Viejo: historic, lively, and tourist-dense

The medieval old quarter, known locally as "Las Siete Calles" (the Seven Streets), is dense with pintxos bars and genuinely charming — and genuinely not the quietest place to actually live, part of the wider Ibaiondo district. Same honest caveat as every old-town neighborhood in this series: worth visiting nightly, worth thinking through before signing a year-long lease directly above it.

## Abando and the Ensanche: Bilbao's central grid

::img[Bilbao Guggenheim riverside]

Abando, including the 19th-century Ensanche grid also sometimes called Albia, is the central business district around Gran Vía and the Guggenheim — Bilbao's most expensive area alongside Indautxu, practical and walkable, the "just live centrally, skip the old town" pick. One naming note worth clearing up: "Ensanche" isn't a separate official district, it's the common name for this grid inside Abando — the same kind of naming layer that trips people up with Madrid's barrio de La Latina.

## Indautxu: upscale and polished

Upscale shopping and residential district adjacent to Abando, Indautxu is the single priciest district in the city by sale price (€5,301/m², against a citywide average of €3,830/m²) — family-friendly and polished rather than historic, the district locals point to when they mean "nice part of town."

## Deusto: riverside and university-adjacent

Home to the University of Deusto, connected to the center by the Deusto Bridge and the "Pottoki" pedestrian footbridge, Deusto is a genuine student and young-professional draw — riverside, more affordable than the central cluster, with a real academic-neighborhood feel rather than a generic student-district one.

## Begoña and Uribarri: the hillside, local-first option

**Begoña**, paired here with neighboring Santutxu, is hillside and traditional, home to the Basílica de Begoña, with funicular access down to the center — a real local, less touristy residential option. **Uribarri**, above Casco Viejo and connected by the Artxanda funicular, is among the more affordable districts in the city while still being genuinely central — worth flagging as underrated precisely because it doesn't show up in typical "best neighborhoods" content the way Casco Viejo or Indautxu do.

## Which neighborhood fits your budget

:::highlight
Indautxu and Abando aren't a toll you pay to live centrally in Bilbao. Uribarri sits a funicular ride from the same center, at one of the lowest price points in the city — the premium in the central grid buys polish and an address, not a monopoly on being close to everything.
:::

If history and pintxos-bar density matter most, Casco Viejo puts you in the middle of it, with the usual noise trade-off. If you want central without the old-town intensity, Abando or Indautxu do the job at a real premium. If you'd rather have space and a quieter, more local feel, Begoña and Uribarri stretch the budget furthest while staying genuinely connected.
