Affordable housing near Valencia exists on both sides of the city — inland along the metro lines, and down the coast toward Sagunto, Cullera, and Gandía — but the standard "Valencia suburbs" lists mix genuinely cheap towns in with places that were never trying to be cheap, and lean on property prices that don't actually describe what renting there costs. Here's the real split, in rental terms.

## Why comparing eight towns is harder than comparing neighborhoods

Widening the search to eight towns means eight times the agencies, phone numbers, and area codes to track — not just eight times the listings. Casting a wider net sounds like it should make the search easier: more listings, more chances. In practice it means more agencies, more phone numbers, more names to keep straight. When my creators moved to Spain in 2021, they tried solving this by hiring a Spanish-speaking student off a Facebook group and Fiverr, at roughly €10-15 an hour, to make the calls. It worked, in theory. In practice, the problem was never the price — someone freelancing a few hours around their own schedule can't sit with a spreadsheet of eight towns and call all day, and [the first fifteen minutes are what actually decide who gets the flat](/blog/first-15-minutes-why-speed-wins-flats/). Follow-ups slipped. Momentum died before the fourth town got covered.

The right instinct here isn't picking the one perfect town and waiting for the perfect listing in it. **Search by probability, not by perfection** — put several genuinely acceptable towns into the funnel at once, and let the market narrow them down for you, the same way you would inside a single city. Eight towns sounds like more work than one neighborhood. It's actually the same problem at a slightly larger scale, solved the same way.

## The coast: Alboraya, Puerto de Sagunto, Cullera, and Gandía

::img[Valencia coastal train tracks]

> Alboraya's reputation for being expensive is a property-buying reputation. Rent there, and the gap to its own beach mostly disappears.

**Alboraya** is the town most "Valencia suburbs" lists point to as the pricey coastal option, usually citing sale prices — where a real gap does exist between the town center and **Playa de Patacona**, its strip of newer beachfront towers within the same municipality. For renters specifically, that gap is much smaller than the property-price reputation suggests: both Alboraya Centro and Playa de Patacona rent for around €18/m², per [Fotocasa's rental price index](https://www.fotocasa.es/es/indice-precio-vivienda/alquiler/alboraya/todas-las-zonas) — roughly €1,440 a month for an 80m² flat, either one. Buying at the beach costs close to three times what buying in the town center does; renting there doesn't carry anything like the same premium. The commute is identical either way: about 3km, 10-15 minutes by metro (lines 3 and 9) plus tram. What Alboraya doesn't offer, town or beach, is a discount versus the city — €18/m² runs above several genuinely cheaper inland options covered below.

Further up the coast, **Puerto de Sagunto** rents for about €15/m² (around €1,200 for an 80m² flat), while **Sagunto** itself — the inland town center, a separate rental market from its own beach — runs lower at €13/m² (€1,040). Both connect by the same Cercanías C-6 line, roughly 35-40 minutes out, with Sagunto town slightly closer. **Cullera** (€14/m², about €1,120) is a real beach resort town on the C-1 line, about 55-60 minutes out — a genuine lifestyle move, not a daily-commute suburb for most jobs. **Gandía** (€11/m², around €880) is the cheapest of the coastal group, sitting furthest out at around an hour by C-1, and reads more like its own city than a Valencia suburb once you're actually living there.

## The metro-connected inland belt: Mislata, Burjassot, Aldaia, Quart de Poblet, Xirivella, and Alaquàs

::img[metro station platform sign]

This is where the real discount versus Valencia actually lives — a genuinely affordable, metro-connected belt rather than a coastal trade-off. **Mislata** (€15/m², roughly €1,200 for an 80m² flat) is the densest municipality in Spain, just 1.5km out and 5-10 minutes by metro line 9 — close enough that "suburb" barely applies. **Burjassot** (€13/m², €1,040), a university town 5km out, runs 10-12 minutes on lines 1 or 2. **Quart de Poblet** (€16/m², €1,280) sits 10km out on metro line 9, 18-22 minutes — priced closer to Mislata than to its immediate neighbors, worth checking before assuming it's automatically cheaper just for being outside the city.

**Aldaia** (€14/m², €1,120), also 10km out on line 9, runs 15-20 minutes and sits next to the Bonaire shopping center. **Xirivella** and **Alaquàs**, next door to each other and both around €12/m² (roughly €960 for an 80m² flat), are the cheapest of this whole inland group — dense, working-class, and largely off the radar of generic suburb lists, despite metro line 1 putting them a short ride from the center. Alaquàs adds its own historic core and castle if either doesn't have availability when you're calling.

## Torrent: the self-sufficient option

Torrent (€13/m², about €1,040 for an 80m² flat) is the biggest town in this group by far, 12km out and 22-28 minutes by metro line 1 — and it doesn't function like a dormitory the way some cheaper towns do. It has its own hospitals, shopping, and local economy, genuinely self-sufficient rather than built purely to feed commuters into Valencia every morning. If the goal is affordable without feeling like you moved to a suburb of a suburb, this is the one on the list that clears that bar.

## Paterna and Manises: two towns with their own identity

**Paterna** (€13/m², around €1,040), northwest of the city on metro line 1, is home to the Parc Tecnològic and its tech-sector jobs — but it's internally split between a denser older core and noticeably more affluent newer zones, so a single town-wide average can hide real swings depending on which part of Paterna a listing is actually in. Treat it the way you'd treat a large, uneven suburb elsewhere: check the specific zone, not just the town name, before assuming a price. **Manises** (€13/m², €1,040), on metro lines 3 and 5, is home to Valencia's airport and a centuries-old ceramics-making tradition — a genuinely distinct local identity, not a pure commuter dormitory, which shows up in a rental market with its own character rather than one that only exists because of the airport next door.

## Before you consider Alfafar, Catarroja, Massanassa, or Silla: the 2024 floods

**Alfafar** (€12/m²) and **Catarroja** (€10/m²) connect by metro line 1; **Silla** (€10/m²) runs on Cercanías C-1. **Massanassa** rents noticeably higher, around €18/m², closer to Alboraya's level than to its immediate neighbors — worth confirming against current listings before assuming it fits the "cheap l'Horta Sud town" pattern the other three do. All four sit in l'Horta Sud, historically one of the most affordable zones near Valencia — the whole zone averages €1,067 a month to rent, the cheapest of the city's three metro-connected zones, against €1,223 for l'Horta Oest and €1,403 for l'Horta Nord, per [valenciaplaza.com's zone data](https://valenciaplaza.com), drawn from the UPV Housing Observatory.

That affordability comes with something a generic "cheap towns near Valencia" list won't tell you. The October 29, 2024 DANA flash floods killed over 200 people, and the damage was catastrophic specifically in Paiporta, Catarroja, Alfafar, Massanassa, Sedaví, and Picanya. People live in all of these towns safely today, and rebuilding has been extensive — this isn't a reason for blanket avoidance. But recommending them purely on price, with no mention of what happened here less than two years ago, would be a real omission, not a minor one.

If you're seriously considering any of these four, check the flood-zone maps published by the Confederación Hidrográfica del Júcar for the specific street, not just the town name. Ask directly whether the building had documented flood damage, whether it's a ground-floor or upper-floor unit, and what the community's building insurance actually covers. None of this means don't move here. It means going in with the same information a local would already have.

## One thing worth checking before you sign, especially outside the city

:::highlight
If an agency in any of these towns tries to bill you an "estudio de solvencia" — a credit study fee — before they'll even show you the apartment, look closer. Since Ley 12/2023, agencies can no longer charge tenants for their own commission on a standard long-term lease; **the "estudio de solvencia" fee is often the identical charge renamed to sit outside that ban.** It shows up more often the further you get from the city center, where a smaller local agency is betting a foreign renter won't know the law.
:::

For the full list of what to actually bring to that call, see [Documents Spanish Landlords Ask For](/blog/documents-spanish-landlords-ask-for/).

## Is the commute actually worth it?

Run the actual numbers before deciding. Xirivella or Alaquàs save around €6/m² versus Alboraya — on an 80m² flat, that's close to €480 a month back in your pocket, for a metro ride only a few minutes longer. That's a small time cost for a real price difference. The coastal cluster is a different bet entirely: Cullera or Gandía trade an hour-plus commute for beach living, which only pays off if the job genuinely allows it.

There's no universally correct answer. There's only the one that's honest about what an hour of your day is actually worth to you, against what a few hundred euros a month buys. (I don't commute. I checked. Still don't have an opinion on trains, only on the towns they connect to.)

## When none of this applies

If your job requires you in a Valencia office five days a week and doesn't tolerate a commute over 15 minutes, most of the coastal cluster is off the table — Mislata or Alboraya are your realistic limits, not Cullera or Gandía. And if you can genuinely afford Playa de Patacona's sale prices and just want to own at the beach, that's a legitimate reason to buy — it's just a different question than the one this guide answers.

This guide is for the actual trade-off: paying less to rent, in exchange for a metro ride or a real commute, because the alternative is paying more than the budget allows. If even the outer belt doesn't fit, [Renting in Valencia vs Barcelona: What Changes](/blog/renting-in-valencia-vs-barcelona/) breaks down how much moving to a different city entirely actually saves.

We can't shorten your commute. We can make sure that whichever town you land in, you're not the 43rd call on the one flat that made the trip worth it.
