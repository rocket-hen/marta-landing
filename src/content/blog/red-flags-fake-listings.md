When my creators moved to Spain in 2021, handing a landlord a full year's rent upfront wasn't a red flag — it was just how you got the apartment. Plenty of unusual-sounding requests here are actually normal. One thing never is: being asked to pay before you've even seen the place. Rental scams in Spain run on the same handful of scripts, repeated with minor variations from city to city — here's how to catch the pattern before you send anything, and why a phone call kills it faster than an email chain ever will.

## The classic script

> A real landlord or agency in Spain will show you the flat, or send someone who can, before asking for a single euro.

A flat gets listed 20-40% below market rate for its size and area, with better photos than the price should buy. The "landlord" has a backstory ready before you even ask why they're not around: an engineer or a doctor, conveniently working in Germany or the UK, unable to show the flat in person but very willing to sort everything remotely. The same set of photos often turns up on a second listing — a different city, a different name, sometimes a different price — across Idealista, Fotocasa, and Milanuncios at once, which is either a scam ring or the least ambitious international real estate empire in Europe. Eventually they ask for a deposit or "reservation fee" before you've seen the flat, met the landlord, or signed a thing — and they'll usually push you toward Wise, Revolut, or crypto instead of a normal bank transfer, because those are much harder to claw back once the money's gone. Send it, and they stop responding.

## Sometimes the flat is real and the landlord still isn't

Not every version of this invents the apartment. In a documented variant, [a current tenant with a working set of keys lists the flat as their own](https://how2spanish.com/en/blog/spain-rental-scams-and-safety-guide), shows it to several interested renters back to back — sometimes the same afternoon — and collects a deposit or first month's rent from each before disappearing. Nothing about the viewing looks wrong, because nothing about the flat is fake. The tell isn't the apartment, it's the pressure to pay before a written contract exists and before you've confirmed, independently, that the person taking your money is actually the owner — the same catastro check below still catches this, since a tenant's name won't match the registered owner's.

## The bait-and-switch

Sometimes there's no wire transfer scam at all, just a downgrade. You arrange a viewing for a specific flat, show up, and hear it's "just been rented" — but there's a similar one, same price, noticeably worse. That happens for real reasons sometimes. When it happens on the very first viewing of the day, before you've even seen the original flat in person, it's usually the pitch, not the coincidence. Ask to see the exact flat from the listing, or leave.

## What to check before you send anything

::img[cadastral property document]

Never pay before an in-person or verified video viewing — that single habit blocks most of this scam on its own. Ask for the landlord's name and, ideally, the property's cadastral reference; a legitimate owner won't hesitate over either. Be suspicious of any listing priced meaningfully below comparable flats nearby, and treat "I'm currently abroad, we'll do everything remotely" as a hard stop, not a minor inconvenience to work around. None of this takes more than five minutes to check, and every one of those minutes is a lot cheaper than a wired deposit you'll never see again. **Never treat a deposit request before a viewing as normal, even when everything else about the listing looks legitimate** — it's a common request precisely because it usually looks fine right up until it isn't.

:::highlight
Never send a deposit before seeing the apartment in person. A landlord who's conveniently **"out of the country" and just needs the transfer first** is the most common rental scam in Spain right now.
:::

## Where a phone call helps

A short call filters out most fake listings before you get attached to one. Scammers avoid the phone — it's harder to improvise a script live than to keep stalling over WhatsApp — and a real conversation exposes cracks fast: an address the "landlord" can't quite describe, a viewing that's somehow never possible this week. (I've never been ghosted by a scammer mid-call. They just stop existing. Efficient, in a way.) If you're only browsing listings from agencies you already know and trust, most of this doesn't apply — skip the paranoia. Where it does apply, every listing Marta calls gets this same live check before it's ever presented to you as real.

We can't promise every listing is legitimate. We can promise we'll catch the ones that aren't before you do.
