A single person in Barcelona can expect to spend €800-1,000 a month excluding rent, and a family of four €2,900-3,500 — but that city-wide average hides a real range depending on where you actually live. Groceries and transport cost roughly the same across the city; rent alone can be double in Eixample versus Nou Barris. This guide breaks down the real monthly cost, and shows how it shifts neighborhood by neighborhood.

## Monthly budget at a glance: single person vs. family of four

Excluding rent, a single person in Barcelona spends €800-1,000 a month, according to [Numbeo's cost-of-living index for Barcelona](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/Barcelona) (€816.8) and HousingAnywhere (€800) — close enough to treat as one range rather than two competing numbers. A family of four runs €2,900-3,500 a month, also excluding rent (Numbeo: €2,946.6; HousingAnywhere: €2,880).

> The figure that trips people up isn't the budget itself — it's whether rent is already baked into it. Half the sources you'll find don't say, and the other half don't agree with each other because of it.

Some sources quote higher totals because they fold rent in from the start — Expatistan's €4,252 family figure, for instance, includes rent, which is why it looks so different from the Numbeo and HousingAnywhere numbers above. One real data point worth keeping in mind: a family of three vlogging their move in 2026 reported spending €2,700 a month total, rent included — a useful anchor for what "living carefully" looks like in practice, not a number to plan a budget around.

## Rent by neighborhood: where the real cost difference is

::img[Barcelona apartment balconies]

A private room in a shared flat runs about €650 a month. A full apartment runs €1,100-1,600+, and which end of that range you land on depends almost entirely on the neighborhood, not the city average. Barcelona's city-wide rent average is €22.8/m², but that number is doing a lot of work hiding a real spread: Nou Barris and Sant Andreu sit around €14-16/m², while Sarrià-Sant Gervasi and L'Eixample run €21-24/m² — for a typical 70m² apartment, that's roughly €1,100 versus €1,575, before you've changed anything else about your life.

These figures assume a standard long-term lease. [Short stays and seasonal contracts run by different rules entirely](/blog/temporary-rental-vs-long-term-lease-spain), often at a markup that makes this whole budget look conservative.

Renting in nearby Valencia runs meaningfully cheaper across the board if the city itself isn't the requirement — [we cover that comparison in detail here](/blog/renting-in-valencia-vs-barcelona). Within Barcelona itself, the same logic applies at a smaller scale: Sant Martí and Gràcia sit in the middle of the range, close enough to the center to not feel like a compromise, cheap enough to matter.

## Groceries, utilities, and transport

Groceries run €300-350 per person a month, and this barely moves by neighborhood — a supermarket in Nou Barris and one in Eixample charge close to the same prices. Utilities (electricity, gas, water) for an average flat run around €100 a month. Public transport costs €25-80 a month depending on whether you buy single tickets as needed or a monthly travel card — the monthly card wins quickly if you're commuting daily, so check current TMB pricing before assuming the cheaper option is actually cheaper for your routine.

This is the part of the budget worth understanding early: rent is where the neighborhood choice pays off. Everything in this section is close to fixed no matter where you live, which is exactly why it's not worth agonizing over.

## Going out: what a couple of nights a week actually costs

::img[bar terrace evening]

A beer at a local bar (a caña or tubo) runs €2.50-4; the same beer from a supermarket costs €0.70-1.30. Tourist-area bars and Irish pubs charge €4-6+, and nightclubs €6-10. A glass of wine runs €4-5, an inexpensive meal out €15-20, and a mid-range three-course dinner for two around €60.

Do the math on two nights out a week — two drinks each, at a normal local bar, four weeks a month — and you land somewhere around €80-130, not counting food. One self-employed expat vlogger reported spending about €50 a month total on nightlife, which reads as unusually disciplined next to that math rather than typical (he's the Kramer of Barcelona budgeting: technically it works, nobody's quite sure how).

## Clothing and everyday shopping

Fashion in Spain runs about 8% cheaper than the EU average, and Zara specifically — designed and produced closer to home — costs 20-30% less than the same items in the US. Budget chains like Primark, Mango Outlet, and Skunkfunk Outlet cover basics locally, and there's a solid second-hand market if you'd rather not pay retail at all.

There's no single authoritative monthly clothing budget the way there is for groceries or transport — anyone who quotes you a precise number is estimating, same as we are. €40-60 a month covers basics for one person; treat it as a planning estimate, not a fact someone measured.

## Per person vs. per family: adjusting the numbers

A single person renting a room in a mid-range neighborhood, budgeting groceries, transport, and a couple of nights out a week, lands close to €1,450-1,650 a month all-in — €650 rent, €800-1,000 living costs. A family of four in a full apartment in a mid-tier neighborhood lands closer to €4,300-5,100 — €1,400-1,600 rent plus the €2,900-3,500 living budget from the top of this guide.

:::highlight
Stop treating the Barcelona city line like a cliff edge. The real budget lever isn't cutting groceries or going out less — it's the neighborhood. Moving from Eixample to Sant Andreu can cut your single biggest cost by 30-40% without changing anything else about how you live.
:::
